Light is slow here

In this park, the speed of light is 5 kilometers per hour — a brisk walk. Just like in the real world, the speed of light is not attainable, only approachable asymptotically. The more you speed up, the more relativistic effects will become apparent: length contraction, time dilation, Terrell rotation, etc. The Doppler effect of light also becomes apparent, with objects in front of you blueshifted, objects behind you redshifted, and transverse Doppler effects visible on objects in motion.

Things to try

  • Accelerate by pressing the Up arrow. Your field of view begins to condense in the direction you are traveling, and becomes blueshifted. If you look behind you by using your mouse to turn, that field of view is redshifted and stretched out. This is relativistic aberration. Press Space to stop any motion, and observe how the field of view returns to normal.
  • The beacons all flash once per second of world time. Walk toward one and the flashing speeds up; walk away and it slows down. Also observe the timers labeled "Your watch" and "World clock", and see time dilation in effect.
  • Stand still and watch the shuttle run back and forth, attaining a maximum speed of 75% of the speed of light. It contracts when it is moving the fastest. And because light from its far end left earlier than light from its near end, it also looks "turned," which is Terrell rotation.
  • The carousel cars move at 75% of the speed of light. They are contracted by their own motion, and you see each one at a different moment in its trip around the ring. The half of the ring running away from you is beamed almost to black while the half coming toward you flares: the relativistic searchlight effect.
  • The Ferris wheel also runs its cars at 75% of the speed of light, but its gondolas hang level the whole way round, so each one is in pure translation and is contracted along whichever way it happens to be going. Follow one car: it is squat at the top and bottom of the ride, where it is travelling horizontally, and pinched at the sides, where it is going straight up or down. The contraction does not change, only its direction does.
  • The wheel stands broadside to the corridor, so run down the corridor past it. The cars at the top of the wheel are going your way and slow to a crawl relative to you — match their 0.75 c and they stop dead — while the cars at the bottom come the other way and pass at 0.96 c, not the 1.5 c you would get by adding. That is Einstein velocity addition, with both answers in front of you at once. The lamp on each car flashes on the same world-time schedule as every other beacon in the park, so you can count the difference.

A note on Doppler shift

The rendering of blueshifts and redshifts is, of course, an approximation. At extreme redshifts, the color fades to black. And at extreme blueshifts (past violet), the color turns to a metallic silver to represent x-rays, then to bright white to represent gamma rays (obviously unphysical, but just to get a sense of it).

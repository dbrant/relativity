Light is slow here

In this park the speed of light is 5 kilometres an hour — a brisk walk. Break into a run and the world in front of you crowds together and turns blue, the world behind you reddens and falls away, and your watch starts losing time against the clocks around you.

Field guide

Everything you see is computed by tracing light back down your past light cone and boosting it into your moving frame — the same arithmetic that applies to starlight, just with c turned down to walking pace so you can outrun it.

Nothing here rubs. Let go of the keys and you keep every scrap of velocity you built up, so you can settle at β = 0.99 and take as long as you like looking around from there. Space is the brake.

Things worth trying

  • Two surveyor's staves lie in the park, both 60 m, banded every 2 m. One runs along the corridor, one across it. Sprint down the corridor and count the bands: only the stave lying along your path loses any.
  • Run hard and look behind you. The entire world astern collapses into a small, dim, red disc — aberration sweeps almost the whole sky into the direction you are travelling.
  • The beacons all flash once per second of world time. Walk toward one and it hurries; walk away and it dawdles. That is the Doppler shift you can count on your fingers.
  • Stand still and watch the shuttle run back and forth. It is genuinely shorter when it is moving fastest — and because light from its far end left earlier than light from its near end, it also looks turned. That apparent twist is Terrell rotation.
  • The carousel cars are contracted by their own motion, and you see each one at a different moment in its trip around the ring. The half of the ring running away from you is beamed almost to black while the half coming toward you flares — the searchlight effect, on a fairground ride.
  • Press G at speed to drop back to a Galilean view. Whatever changes when you do is exactly the relativity.
  • Let the clocks run apart, then hit R. Your watch is the one that falls behind — every second of yours is γ seconds of theirs.

What is being approximated

The Doppler shift is real spectral work: each surface colour is treated as a reflectance spectrum under a 5800 K sun, shifted by the Doppler factor, and re-integrated against the CIE 1931 colour matching functions. Objects fade to black at extreme speed because the sun has no light left to give at those wavelengths. Beaming uses the bolometric D⁴; the tone mapping afterwards is a film curve, not physics.
